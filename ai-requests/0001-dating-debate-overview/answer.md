## Full answer, verbatim

The two main positions on the date of Revelation are usually argued as
follows:

**Domitianic date (90s AD).** The strongest external witness is Irenaeus of
Lyons, writing around 180 AD in *Against Heresies* 5.30.3, who states that
John's vision "was seen not very long time ago, but almost in our day,
towards the end of Domitian's reign." This is the earliest patristic
testimony on the question and is usually taken as the default scholarly
position. Eusebius later repeats this tradition in his *Ecclesiastical
History*. Clement of Alexandria's account in *Quis Dives Salvetur* 42 — the
story of John returning from "the island" after the death of "the tyrant" to
reconcile with a young man who had turned to banditry — is often cited as
indirectly supporting a Domitian-era exile and return, though this
identification is not made fully explicit in the text itself.

**Neronic date (60s AD).** The main arguments here are internal: passages
like Revelation 17:10 ("five have fallen, one is, the other has not yet
come") are read by proponents of this view as pointing to an emperor
reigning in the late 60s, and the Temple-related imagery in Revelation 11 is
read by some as implying the Jerusalem Temple was still standing (destroyed
in 70 AD). For a developed defense of the Neronic date with a direct
response to the Domitianic consensus, see Michael A. Thornbury, "The
Neronic Redating of the Apocalypse," Journal of Early Christian Studies 12
(2004), 45-67, which works through both the internal evidence and Irenaeus's
testimony in detail.
